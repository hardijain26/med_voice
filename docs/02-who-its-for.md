# 02 — Who It's For

Med-Voice is built around one primary user and several people who care about them. Knowing these personas keeps every product decision honest: *would this actually help Kamala?*

## Primary persona — The senior patient

**"Kamala, 72"**
- Takes 3–4 medicines a day for blood pressure, diabetes, and a recent infection.
- Reads slowly, prefers her mother tongue (Tamil) over English, and squints at small print.
- Has a smartphone her son set up, but mostly uses it for calls, photos, and video chats.
- **Her pain:** She's afraid of taking the wrong pill or the wrong amount, so she either over-relies on family or, on bad days, skips medicine entirely.
- **What she needs:** Something that *tells* her, out loud and in Tamil, exactly what to take and when — and reminds her so she doesn't have to remember.

## Secondary persona — The caregiver

**"Arjun, 40, Kamala's son"**
- Lives in another city; calls his mother daily, partly to check on her medicines.
- Worries constantly about mix-ups, especially after a hospital visit adds new prescriptions.
- **His pain:** He's the human reminder system, and it's exhausting and unreliable.
- **What he needs:** Confidence that his mother is guided correctly even when he's not on the phone — and the ability to set this up for her quickly.

## Tertiary persona — The occasional helper

**"A neighbor, a nurse, a pharmacist"**
- Anyone who briefly assists a senior and wants to hand them something that keeps working after they leave.
- **What they need:** A tool simple enough to set up in one visit.

## Who it is *not* built for (today)

Being clear about non-users keeps the product focused:

- **Not a clinical tool for professionals.** It simplifies *for the patient*; it is not a pharmacist's dispensing system or a substitute for professional judgment.
- **Not a diagnosis or advice engine.** It explains an existing prescription. It does not decide *whether* a medicine is right for someone.
- **Not a full medication-management record system** (yet) — see [Roadmap Ideas](./07-roadmap.md).

## The accessibility lens

Because the core user often has reduced vision, limited literacy in the label's language, and low comfort with apps, three principles are non-negotiable:

1. **Voice over text** — the important information is always available by ear.
2. **Fewest possible taps** — capture, understand, listen.
3. **No jargon, ever** — "after breakfast," not "post-prandial."
