# 07 — Roadmap Ideas

These are *suggestions*, not commitments — a menu a product manager can prioritize against user value and effort. They build directly on what already exists.

## Near-term — strengthen the core (trust & safety)

1. **Confirm-the-reading step.** After scanning, show the extracted medicine name and dose and ask "Is this right?" A tiny addition that dramatically de-risks the most serious failure mode.
2. **Label the fallback clearly.** Make the safe-default guidance unmistakably a *general example* with a "try again" prompt, so it's never confused with the user's real prescription (see [Limitations & Risks](./06-limitations-and-risks.md)).
3. **Capture guidance.** On-screen help to get a clear photo ("move closer," "hold steady," "reduce glare"), lifting first-scan success.
4. **Plain-language privacy statement.** Build the trust the product depends on.

## Mid-term — deepen the value

5. **Multi-medicine management.** Let a user hold several prescriptions and see one combined daily schedule — "here's everything you take today."
6. **Caregiver companion mode.** A guided flow for a family member to set up and monitor a loved one's medicines remotely, addressing the caregiver persona directly.
7. **Smarter reminders.** "Did you take it?" confirmations, gentle re-nudges, and refill reminders before a medicine runs out.
8. **More languages and native-speaker review.** Expand beyond 11, with a quality pass per language — especially for safety warnings.
9. **Accessibility polish.** Larger-text mode, higher-contrast visuals, and slower/adjustable voice speed for hard-of-hearing users.

## Longer-term — expand the platform

10. **Pharmacy & clinic partnerships.** Offer Med-Voice as a branded service at the point of dispensing (the existing partner hand-off capability is a natural foundation).
11. **Drug-interaction awareness.** With appropriate clinical safeguards, warn when combined medicines may conflict — a significant step that requires careful validation.
12. **Offline-first mode.** Reduce dependence on connectivity for users with unreliable internet.
13. **Family sharing & adherence reports.** A simple weekly summary a caregiver can receive ("Mom took 13 of 14 doses this week").

## How to prioritize (a simple lens)

For each idea, ask three questions:

1. **Does it protect the user?** (Safety items win by default in a health product.)
2. **Does it move the north-star metric** — correctly-guided doses acted on? ([Success Metrics](./05-success-metrics.md))
3. **What's the effort vs. the reach?** Favor small changes that de-risk or unlock many users.

A reasonable first sprint: items **1–4** (near-term trust & safety), because they are low-effort, high-safety, and increase the credibility needed for everything after.
