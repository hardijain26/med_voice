Product overview · for non-technical readers & PMs

A prescription label most people can't read, turned into spoken help they can.

Med-Voice reads a prescription from a photo and tells seniors — in their own language — which medicine to take, how much, and exactly when. No tiny print. No medical jargon. No typing.

The problem

The instructions are right there. Most people just can't use them.

Poor medication adherence — wrong doses, missed doses, quietly giving up — is one of the biggest preventable causes of avoidable hospital visits. A confusing label is often where it starts.

What the senior faces

  • Print too tiny to read without a magnifier.
  • Clinical shorthand ("PO BID p.c.") nobody says aloud.
  • Printed in a language they don't read comfortably.
  • "Twice daily" — but no sense of when, or with food.

What Med-Voice gives back

  • Nothing to read — it speaks the instructions aloud.
  • Warm, everyday words: "1 tablet after breakfast."
  • Their own language, pronounced correctly.
  • Clear times, tied to meals, with voice reminders.

How it works

Three taps: point, understand, listen.

The whole experience is built around a user who reads slowly, dislikes typing, and would rather listen. Everything complex happens out of sight.

Point the camera

Aim the phone at the bottle, box, or paper. That's the only input — no typing, no searching for the medicine by name.

Med-Voice reads it

AI extracts the medicine and dose, rewrites the instructions in plain language, translates them, and splits the day into meal-based dose times.

It speaks & reminds

The instructions are read aloud in the chosen language, shown as a simple schedule, and backed by voice reminders at each dose time.

A quiet piece of cleverness: some phones mispronounce non-English scripts, so Med-Voice also produces a phonetic version in English letters. The phone's voice speaks it smoothly — the user only ever hears the correct pronunciation.

★ North star — weekly correctly-guided doses acted on
Accurate guidance delivered and acknowledged as taken. It combines reach, quality, and outcome; every feature should move this number.

This is health-adjacent data for a vulnerable group. Measure with restraint: collect the minimum, prefer aggregate counts over anything that identifies a person, and treat trust as the product.

Honest limits

Risks & how they're handled

  • HighAI misreads the label. Mitigate with a "does this look right?" confirmation of medicine & dose, plus capture guidance.
  • MedAI offline. Fallback gives safe guidance — but it must be clearly marked a general example, never the user's real label.
  • MedTranslation nuance. Warm, simple phrasing today; add native-speaker review per language, especially for warnings.
  • HighHealth-data privacy. Transparent permissions; add a plain-language policy on what happens to a photo.

Med-Voice explains an existing prescription. It is not medical advice, not a diagnosis tool, and not a substitute for a doctor or pharmacist.

Where it could go

Roadmap ideas

  • NowConfirm-the-reading step. A tiny addition that de-risks the most serious failure mode.
  • NowCapture guidance & privacy note. Lift first-scan success; build the trust the product depends on.
  • NextMulti-medicine & caregiver mode. One combined daily schedule; a setup flow for family members.
  • NextPharmacy partnerships. Offer Med-Voice at the point of dispensing, using the existing hand-off capability.

Prioritize by three questions: does it protect the user, does it move the north star, and what's the effort vs. the reach?
